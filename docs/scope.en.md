# Details and limitations

[日本語](scope.md) ・ [README](../README.en.md)

How each feature in the README works in detail, what is sent to Claude when you try your own text, and the current scope and limitations.

## Details

- **Read notices:** Claude reads text you paste or dictate with your phone keyboard's voice input, or text transcribed from a photo or PDF of a handout and checked by you, and extracts events, belongings, submissions, schedule changes, and more. Each item stays linked to the original text, and the Notices screen marks the sentences that have items or need a check.
- **Show decision deadlines:** From the notice's deadlines, rules the family has registered (after-school care forms, leave requests, how far ahead to ask a grandparent), and the usual week (work, commute, and pickup duties), Maniau estimates by when each option must be chosen and shows the next task. For purchases, it shows when a shop on the way home is open. Belongings with a stated day of use that you said are at home, or that have been bought, appear on the Today screen from the day before that day. If one turns out to be missing, you can change the answer there until the end of that day (before the preparation deadline, Maniau plans the purchase; after it, Maniau shows both adults that the item is missing). When no way to make it in time is found with what the family has registered, Maniau says so (finding another way is up to the family).
- **Choose:** For each arrangement, Maniau lists the options you can still choose with how long each one remains available, and shows a rule-based suggestion (the lightest option when leave, asking others, cost and extra tasks are combined with rough weights) with its reason. For each option it shows what would happen if you chose it (the first task and who does it, waiting for a reply, whom to tell about a cancellation, other tasks whose times move), computed with the same calculation without recording anything, and it also shows new violations found by the verifier. An option's decision deadline is the last moment any adult can still make it, so when an option no longer fits your own time, Maniau shows it as proposing the tasks to an adult whose time it fits, and when doing it yourself means starting earlier, it adds who could still make it later and by when. If what you saw (dates, tasks, who does them, cancellations, tasks whose times move) changes before you choose, Maniau shows it again before recording the choice. Options the family is pursuing (main and backup) are placed together so they do not overlap for the same adult, keeping accepted times where possible. Steps already done, and a backup no longer pursued because the main option was confirmed, take no time (the time a step was done is not recorded, so its planned time is not kept as when it was done). When Maniau cannot find non-overlapping times for an option's tasks, it does not overlap them: it leaves them without a time and asks you to review the options, who does them, or the times. You can change the choice later.
- **Make clear who does what:** Maniau proposes each task to one adult and records whether they take it on or decline. Requests to grandparents or after-school care keep "awaiting a reply" separate from "confirmed". For a request awaiting a reply, “What if they say no?” runs the same calculation as if they had said no, without recording anything: whether the backup takes over, or, if you would need to choose again, the suggested option, by when you need their reply to still make it, and each remaining option's decision deadline. Tasks in an option the family is pursuing that have no one assigned appear on every adult's screen. Forms to hand in from a notice ("submit by the 27th") also appear on every adult's screen as a task with its due date until someone takes it. In both cases, if no one has taken the task about a day before its deadline (for an option's task, the option's decision deadline), Maniau sends a notification to each adult (each person needs to turn on notifications to receive it).
- **Adapt to changes:** When a postponement or other change arrives, Maniau removes preparations that are no longer needed and drafts cancellation messages for people who were already asked. When you correct a date that was read from a notice, a reply to a request for the old day does not settle the new day, and you can ask again for the new day (after you do, telling them about the old day becomes a cancellation task). If something a request relied on changes after it was sent and the person needs to be checked with, Maniau keeps the backup until then.
- **Handle other things to do:** What the family says (“someone needs to be home from 8:00 to 18:00 tomorrow”) and the attendance or applications a notice asks for go through the same flow as child care (choosing a way, tasks, taking on, requests, cancellation). Items read from a notice stay out of the plan until the family checks them against the original text. The family chooses how to proceed from ways it has registered. Claude can also suggest ways (these are inferred, and don’t become ways until the family corrects and saves them in the form).
- **Update in a few words:** From a short update such as "Grandma says 12/7 is fine", Maniau finds the matching request or task and suggests what to record. For a what-if such as "What if Grandma says no?", it records nothing and offers the “What if they say no?” calculation instead. For a change to one adult's day, such as “What if I take 12/7 off?” or “Wednesday I'm in a meeting until 7 pm”, it shows who, which day and what changes as editable fields, records nothing, and shows what the same calculation would change (assumptions the message didn't state are listed separately, and the day is recorded only when you tap “Record this day”; you can also add one from Family). Nothing is recorded until you tap (demo).
- **Around us (first step):** Under “Around us” in Family, Maniau keeps facilities and services near home, and things that must be done once before using them (such as registering in advance). Your family enters whether each is done. Options with a step not confirmed as done are not recommended, and their cards say “Beforehand: …” (you can still choose them). Options that use a place your family marked “Don’t use” are not recommended either; the other options are compared and recommended. The demo’s sick-child care room and its advance registration are made-up rules for the demo.
- **Look around us:** In “Around us”, “Look around us” has Claude search the web for sick-child care, temporary childcare and family support in the home’s area (ward and station level), read the pages, and suggest candidates. Only candidates whose cited sentence (a quote Claude gives as its basis) appears in the text of a page Claude read are shown (code checks this after normalizing character width and spaces, not whether the sentence supports the summary; sentences taken from the page as quotes are used only for the check and are not shown or kept; a summary is one sentence written by Claude and may contain the page’s words or sentences, but a summary that is a page sentence, or that contains a cited sentence, is left out when that sentence is 8 or more normalized characters); whether the page is official or still correct is for you to check at the link. Claude’s summaries are in Japanese (the survey asks Claude in Japanese, also when the screen is in English). Each candidate first shows what was found; “Decide how your family treats it” opens the choices. Whether advance registration is needed is shown as Claude’s reading (to check on the page), and your family chooses “needed”, “same registration”, “not needed” or “not sure yet”. Only what your family adds with “Add as a candidate for us” goes into your household information. A way that asks that place is made by copying an existing way (the original way stays as it is; the old place’s hours, cost and deadlines aren’t copied — you check this place’s page and enter them, and choose about advance registration again on that form). The demo shows a result recorded from one real look at Toyosu, Koto ward on 2026-11-05; “Ask Claude to look again now” works up to 3 times per demo. It is not available for family households yet. Regular re-checks are not built yet.
- **Usual stores (family preferences):** In “Around us”, your family marks each of your stores as “Usual”, “Candidate” or “Don’t use” (Claude and new searches never change this). Shopping goes first to a store your family picked for that item; otherwise to a usual store, even if another store could be reached sooner. Stores marked “Don’t use” are never used. If there’s no time to stop at your usual store, Maniau doesn’t quietly move the errand to another store; Today says why, and your family chooses. Before you save a change, you see how the store and the decision deadline change. The demo has two stores that sell children’s clothes (a made-up one near the station was added), with the clothing store near Toyosu Station marked usual. Preferences aren’t used yet for recommending ways (such as a usual sick-child care room).

## Try your own text

When the server has `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` set (as an environment variable or in `.env` at the repository root), the Notices screen shows a box for your own text. Text you paste, or dictate with your phone keyboard's voice input, is read with the Claude API, and items that can be planned are added to the plan. Sentences with extracted items, and sentences that need a check, are marked as added to the plan, shown in the schedule, read only (no reminders yet), or needing a check.

You can also add a photo or PDF of a handout or a contact notebook. Claude transcribes the text as written, in reading order, into the box; compare it with the photo, fix anything that differs, and then read it. The photo is kept neither on the server nor in the records, and the notice is marked as transcribed from a photo. You can send one photo, or a PDF of a few pages (about 5 pages, up to 4 MB), at a time. Before transcribing, the input size is estimated with Anthropic's free token-counting endpoint, and anything too large is refused without calling the transcription (the photo or PDF is sent for this estimate too, so it still counts as one use).

With the same setting, a Quick update box appears at the top of the Today screen. Say that a reply came, that you asked or submitted something, that you finished or can no longer do a task, or that you do (or don't) have an item at home, and Maniau finds the matching request, task or question, suggests what to record, and records it when you tap (when there are others of the same kind, it doesn't record and points you to the cards on Today; see "Current scope and limitations" below). If the item changed after it was shown (for example, the other adult recorded it), nothing is recorded and the current state is shown again. Ask about the plan ("What do we need on 12/5?") and it lists the matching items and their status. Ask a what-if ("What if Grandma says no?") and it suggests nothing to record; it shows the requests waiting for a reply, and tapping one shows the same calculation as “What if they say no?” (nothing is recorded). When Claude can't tell a real reply from a what-if, or reads one update as mixing both, it offers neither and asks you to say them separately (it can misread, so check any suggestion before tapping).

With the same setting, you can ask Claude for ideas from a to-do's screen (“Ask Claude for ideas”). Add your thoughts (such as “Grandma might be able to help”) and Claude suggests up to 3 ways that combine the household's adults, helpers, places and current ways, and points out a current way when it is enough. The ideas are inferred, and getting them changes nothing. Put an idea into the Add a way form, correct it, and save it after seeing what the change would do. Fields Claude couldn't tell (who does it, who to ask, whether it covers the time, and so on) can't be saved until the family chooses them. Prices, how quickly others reply, deadline rules and the hours something can cover have no fields in Claude's ideas; the family enters them.

On a server that also has map keys, you can ask Claude for search words when looking for a store (“Ask Claude for search words”). The words are a guess; Claude isn't asked until you tap, and getting them searches nothing. Google is searched only when the family checks the words (editing them if needed) and taps Search.

The text, the items already recorded in that demo (including what family members said) and open questions are sent to Anthropic. Photos and PDFs are sent to estimate their size and to be transcribed. For quick updates, the update, the demo's (fictional) family and helper names, and the current items, tasks and requests (including the people and places involved) are sent. For store search words, only the item name is sent. For way ideas, the to-do (what, when, place and the original text), your thoughts and who wrote them, the names of the adults, helpers and places, and the names of current ways and their steps are sent (other household details, such as the usual week, children and opening hours, are not; anything written in the text and names that are sent, even an address or a schedule, goes as written). Use made-up text or photos, or remove anything that identifies a person or place. Each demo allows 5 notice readings, 5 photo transcriptions, 20 quick updates, 5 requests for way ideas and 10 requests for store search words; each server process allows 100 in total per day (UTC), and 2 at a time. A server running in family mode offers no demo readings, transcriptions, quick updates, way ideas or store search words. In a family-mode trial household, the same things (that household's) are sent to Anthropic. Each household allows 100 per day (UTC, notices, quick updates and way ideas together), 1 at a time.

## Current scope and limitations

- Adding a notice as text or a photo works in the demo with an API key set. In family mode, only trial households (households marked as test in the database, for fictional families) can read text notices and quick updates (not photos yet; real family material is not entered yet). Photo transcription has been checked only with a synthetic photo of a printed contact notebook; handwriting and photos of real handouts have not been checked yet.
- Family mode (identity checks with Cloudflare Access, Postgres storage, and Web Push notifications) is implemented. It has not yet been verified on a real device together with an iPhone home-screen app. A push provider accepting a notification does not prove that a person received or read it.
- Do not enter real family data until authentication, deletion, notifications, and related safeguards have been checked in the actual environment. See the [foundation design](design/02-foundation.md) for the requirements.
- Quick updates can record a reply, that a request was sent or submitted, done, can no longer do, decline, cancellation sent, and answers to open questions (including whether you have an item at home). Reassigning tasks and recording more than one thing at once are not yet supported. For changes to the usual week, only one adult's single day (day off, home for a while, out for a while, work end time) is supported, and it opens the details screen without recording anything. Ongoing changes, changes for several people or days, pick-up–only changes, working from home and plans past midnight are not yet supported. A day where someone would go home in the middle of work or a commute (travel to and from work can't be worked out), or a day with a pick-up after work whose work end time changes (what happens to the pick-up isn't known), is not calculated, and the screen says why. For time out, include where you go and the travel in that time. The date is filled in only when the date read matches the date the code works out from the message's own wording (today, tomorrow, the day after tomorrow, 12/7, December 7, next Monday and so on). For other wording, wording that could mean more than one day (such as “来週の月曜” said on a Sunday, “Friday” said on a Friday, or “next Friday”), or when the two disagree, the date is left empty for you to enter on the screen. A date word inside a name (such as “明日” in “明日香”) can still be misread unless it is the name of someone in the family, so the details screen shows your original message next to what was read. A what-if in a quick update covers a request waiting for a reply being turned down, and a change to one adult's day (what-ifs such as an adult not getting the day off or the event being postponed are not yet supported). Tasks of options the family hasn't chosen yet (candidates kept for comparison) and tasks no one has taken yet (such as forms to hand in) can't be recorded by a quick update either; choose the option, or decide who does it on the Today screen, first. A quick update records something only when nothing else of the same kind could be meant: the only task in the plan, the only request it could apply to, or the only open question it could answer. Otherwise it doesn't record it, even if the titles, names, items or dates look clearly different, and you record it from the card on Today (text and the way dates are written can look the same while being different, so they are not compared). In the demo, tasks and answers are always recorded from the cards. In a family-mode trial household, replies to requests for a to-do, and telling someone such a request is cancelled, aren't recorded by a quick update either; record them on the request card on Today, which shows what you sent.
- Parent actions in homework (signing, marking, and so on) and parents' duties become tasks. Whether a duty is our turn is inferred from how its audience is written (our child's name, or only other families' names) and from the duty roster in the same notice. This can be wrong (for example, for a row written with a family name), so saying “Not our turn” on any duty card takes it out of the tasks (if the other adult has taken a duty on, they say it), and “… is our turn” on the source screen reverses that. A duty read as another family's can also be made a task with “… is our turn”. Saying this does not confirm a guessed reading (a guessed line is not added to the plan yet).
- Only times written in the sentence an item points to are used. If a time read from a notice can't be found in that sentence, Maniau doesn't use it and asks you to check the sentence (a deadline is estimated at 8:00 that morning and only its day is shown; a duty whose start time couldn't be confirmed is not given a time, and reminders treat the start of that day as the estimate). A deadline read as a day only is also flagged for checking when its sentence contains something that looks like a time (a clock time, “morning”, “evening”, “before school”, and so on), since the time may have been missed. The date of a deadline such as “by tomorrow” is worked out in code from the day the notice arrived, and a reading that differs is shown as a conflict. A bare “9時” with no AM/PM or 24-hour marker is accepted as either 9:00 or 21:00. Times read from a notice can be corrected on screen only for “to do” items (see “To do” items below); for everything else, the family checks the original sentence and decides.
- Events in notices from lessons and clubs become drop-off and pick-up tasks only when the household information gives a child a drop-off and pick-up rule (whether drop-off, pick-up, or both are needed). The rule can't be changed on screen yet; in the demo, 陽菜 has one. In a household with two or more children, Maniau can't yet tell whose event it is, so it makes no tasks. Each task is a round trip around the written start (drop-off) or end (pick-up), estimated at 30 minutes each way (the route is not checked; maps are used only for errands on the way home). A drop-off or pick-up with no written time becomes a task for that day. If the end is not after the start, or the source says “next day” or similar, the pick-up goes on the next day when that is within 12 hours. If the pick-up day still can't be decided (for example, only the end is written, or the end time couldn't be read), Maniau doesn't show the event's day as the pick-up day and asks you to check the date and time in the original text (no notification is sent for that pick-up). “Not needed this time” on a card removes it (if the other adult has taken it on, they remove it), and the source screen can reverse that. Arranging care when a drop-off or pick-up clashes with work (asking Grandma, for example) is not done yet.
- If something you took on is no longer needed because a later notice cancelled it, a condition changed, or the family chose another way, it appears at the top of your Today screen, and you are notified before you would start (or, if it has already started, until it ends). If the original notice was re-read after you took it on (including when the item is no longer found or reads as something else), the original notice was deleted, or a change to the household information means the task is no longer made, Maniau doesn't say it is no longer needed; it asks you to check whether it still is. “Got it” releases you from it. If it becomes needed again before that, Maniau asks again whether you'll take it on. Maniau can't confirm that a notification arrived or was read.
- In “Our family”, you can see the family information Maniau calculates with (people, a usual week, places and shops, ways to cover). For now you can edit the times and kinds of the usual week, shop opening hours, and the deadline rules of the ways to cover. Before saving, the same calculation shows what the change would do to the plan (when the child needs looking after, decision times, task times, assignees and shops, tasks to re-confirm, and so on); points it couldn't check are listed as such, not reported as fine.
- When a notice leaves a decision to the family (“if you will attend”, “those taking part”, and so on), Maniau asks it as “For the family to decide” only when the answer changes what is needed, and lists what each choice would require, with deadlines. Until you decide, the related tasks aren't listed for someone to take on, and Maniau doesn't ask whether you have the related items at home. Notifications start 26 hours before the earliest of those deadlines. If the question or the related items change after you answer (a re-read or a correction), Maniau asks again and shows your earlier answer. A decision that matters only through another item's effect (such as a cancellation) isn't asked yet; those tasks stay visible as conditional.
- “To do” items: what the family says (“someone must be home from 8:00 to 18:00 tomorrow”) and actions a notice asks of the family (“one adult per household, please attend”, “households wishing to use it, please apply at the counter”) are handled as the same kind of item. There are two shapes: someone is needed during a time window, or something must be done by a deadline. From Today, “Add something to do” takes the family's own words and the fields (what, shape, date, times, for whom) and shows what will change before saving. When reading a notice, Maniau doesn't guess who an item is for and doesn't invent times that aren't written. Deadline wording (candidate dates, negated dates, “before school”, and so on) is too varied for code alone to confirm, so nothing is arranged and no tasks are made until the family compares the reading with the original text (the whole notice, with the sentences it is based on in bold) on a Today card and chooses “It is right” (only when the reading is complete), “Fix”, “Misread”, or “Not needed for us” (readings that may not match the original text appear on the same card with a note saying so). The card also shows the condition as read (with its “and”, “or”, and “not” intact), which open question it answers with what, and what (and from when) it replaces or calls off, naming the other notice when these come from one; a reading that can't be shown in a way you can tell apart can't be confirmed (it can still be removed). If such an item was read as replacing or calling off an earlier one, the earlier one is shown as uncertain until the family confirms. When that notice, or a notice its condition, answer, or replaced item comes from, is read again, the family is asked to check again. Even if the date read has passed, or the condition read makes it unnecessary for now, the card stays until the family checks it, with a note saying so (so that a misread date or condition can't hide something still needed; it can be removed with “Not needed for us”). The family chooses how to proceed from ways they registered (steps, who must reply, the hours each covers, costs); no tasks are made until they choose. An option with a step that must be done at a fixed place (such as a counter open only on weekday daytimes) gets no decision deadline when the usual week never puts anyone there; Maniau does not call it too late, says the option includes a step the usual week has no time for, and does not suggest it (demo example: for renewing the after-school club for next year with the ward, it suggests the ward website, not the counter). For a time window, Maniau says it is covered only for the hours covered by a family member taking it on or an outside party's “yes”, and shows the remaining hours (when a place is written, a family member taking it on doesn't count as covering it). Request messages are drafted only for steps the other party covers, and cancellation messages to places don't include what was asked (it may contain a child's name). If a reading is still unchecked an hour after it was made, the person who added the notice gets one notification with no details (again if a confirmation lapses and the card returns; at night it waits until morning; it doesn't promise the deadline can still be met). Both adults get a notification with no details: from 26 hours before the decision deadline when a way still needs to be chosen as the main one, from 26 hours before the date (the window's start or the deadline) when no way has been chosen yet, and from 26 hours before a time window until it ends while some hours have no one confirmed to cover them (before a way is chosen, its advance steps, such as booking the day before, aren't known, so this can be too late). The family adds, edits and stops using ways in “Our family” or on a to-do's screen (seeing what the change would do before saving; stopping a way doesn't change what is already chosen, nor its tasks or requests). A person or place can't be added, or renamed, to share a name with another (you couldn't tell them apart). A to-do in the plan can be changed with “Edit details” or “Mark as not needed” (editing means choosing a way again and checking with anyone already asked; marking it as not needed sends a cancellation to anyone already asked). Claude's way ideas change nothing until the family puts one into the form, corrects it and saves it. The text of an idea (its name, reason and what to check) can't be checked by code; don't treat prices or times written there as checked facts. Claude's ideas have only been checked a few times, with made-up examples.
- Stores are currently matched only for clothing and groceries. For other items (a handkerchief, paints, and so on) with a known date, Maniau asks whether you have one at home and, if not, says it couldn't tell which store sells it (no time to buy it is planned). In the demo on a server with map keys, from that card or an errand card you can search for stores near your home station and pick one as the store for that item (within a 15-minute walk of the station, up to 5, nearest first). The search words, as the family checked or edited them, are sent to Google as typed, only when you tap Search (don't include people's names or addresses). You see what changes before saving the store, and only its place ID and the family's name for it are kept in the household details (Google's store name and address are only shown). Picking another store removes the item from the store picked before.
- If only the time changes on the same day after you asked someone, Maniau only shows that the person who asked should check (there is no action yet to ask again for the new time or to record that it was checked).
- Joint placement covers the steps not yet done of options the family is pursuing for child care (the main option and backups still being pursued). Shopping tasks are not yet placed together with them. Maniau tries every order for up to four options; with more, it places them once in a fixed order. It tries orders of whole options, not interleaving the steps of different options, so it can miss a non-overlapping placement that exists (the tasks are then left without a time and shown). In no case does it claim that there is no solution or that a placement is the best.
- Times and travel durations are estimates. On a server with map keys, demo errands on the way home use walking times from the station to the store (each way, looked up separately) and the store's opening hours, looked up from Google Maps for each request. Within 7 days of the lookup, the hours for that day are checked; beyond that, the usual hours are used and marked as an estimate (special closures on the day are not checked; if a purchase only fits the usual hours, or the route to a picked store can't be looked up, Maniau doesn't say the chance has passed and shows it on Today as not yet decidable). When the lookup fails, the household's estimates (prepared examples in the demo) are used and labeled as such. Hours and routes from Google are not kept and are not sent to Claude. The proposal an adult saw when taking on a task (times, walking minutes and the store's place ID) stays only in the demo server's memory. Family households (which save their records) don't use map values yet. Inventory, actual opening, how easy a route is, and arrival times are not guaranteed.
